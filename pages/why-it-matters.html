Why it matters

Knowing the shape of a molecule is often the first step to understanding it, or to designing something that fits it.

Medicine and drug discovery

Many drugs work by fitting into a pocket on a protein, like a key in a lock. If researchers can predict the shape of a target protein, and how a candidate molecule might bind, they can narrow down which ideas are worth testing in the lab. Boltz-2's binding affinity estimates aim at exactly this step.

Understanding biology

Shapes help explain how proteins work, how diseases such as those caused by faulty proteins arise, and how living things differ. Fast predictions let more scientists explore more questions.

Why open source matters

Because Boltz is openly released, researchers and companies can inspect it, run it themselves and build on it. This helps results be checked and reproduced by others.

Things to keep in mind

  • Predictions can be wrong, especially for unusual molecules.
  • A structure alone does not make a medicine. Testing in the lab and in clinical trials is still essential.
  • Models improve quickly, so check the official sources for the latest.
